# Nation Article Authoring SOP

> **Use this SOP when:** You create or revise a nation, state, dominion, kingdom, republic, confederation, or comparable polity.
>
> **Result:** The article explains how the polity functions, what pressures shape it, and where stories can emerge.
>
> **First action:** Define the article's era, geographic scope, and current authoring purpose.

Follow [[Documentation Writing Standard]] for operational wording.

## Purpose

Create a usable polity article without turning worldbuilding into an encyclopaedic completion exercise.

Connect geography, population, resources, culture, institutions, history, and conflict where those connections affect creative decisions.

## Before you start

1. Confirm that one existing note does not already describe the polity.
2. Set the controlled era or Universal scope.
3. Identify the article's immediate purpose.
4. Open the most authoritative notes for neighbouring places, predecessor states, and major institutions.
5. Record unresolved contradictions as tasks instead of silently choosing an answer.

## Choose the readiness level

Choose **Publication ready** when readers need a clear, coherent overview.

Choose **Reference ready** when a sourcebook, story, collaborator, or major continuity decision depends on deeper systems.

Do not require reference readiness only because optional questions remain unanswered.

## Procedure

### 1. Establish identity and scope

1. State what the polity is.
2. State where and when it exists.
3. State the feature that most strongly distinguishes it.
4. Identify its current form of rule without treating the label as the complete political system.
5. Separate public identity from practical reality when they differ.

### 2. Connect the polity to place

1. Identify the terrain, climate, waterways, and routes that shape settlement and movement.
2. Identify important boundaries, chokepoints, surplus zones, extraction areas, and logistical shadows.
3. State which geographical constraints the polity has solved.
4. State which constraints remain politically or economically important.
5. Link major regions, settlements, routes, and Atlas maps when they exist.

Use [[Worksheets/012-W03 - Resource Economy and Logistics Worksheet|Resource Economy and Logistics Worksheet]] when the map or economy lacks a clear causal structure.

### 3. Explain population and ordinary life

1. Identify the main settlement pattern.
2. Explain how food, water, shelter, fuel, and essential materials reach the population.
3. Identify the work that sustains ordinary households.
4. State where wealth and hardship are concentrated.
5. Record only population figures that have a clear source or documented assumption.

Do not present an estimate as a measured canon fact.

### 4. Explain power

1. Identify the formal institutions.
2. Identify the people or groups with practical leverage.
3. State what each power centre controls.
4. State why others accept, tolerate, or fear its authority.
5. Identify the visible currency of power.
6. Identify the force, dependency, legitimacy, or threat that supports it.
7. Identify one credible fracture point.

Use [[Worksheets/012-W02 - Political System and Fracture Worksheet|Political System and Fracture Worksheet]] when the government label does not explain how decisions are enforced.

### 5. Explain resources and exchange

1. Identify important local resources and capabilities.
2. Identify critical imports and dependencies.
3. Identify who controls production, transport, storage, or specialist knowledge.
4. Trace important routes and chokepoints.
5. State what happens when one major dependency fails.
6. Identify how the resource system affects diplomacy, class, conflict, or expansion.

Use conditional sections. Do not force every polity to discuss metals, salt, coal, shipping, or heavy industry.

### 6. Build a coherent culture

1. Identify the environmental, political, exchange, historical, and belief pressures that matter.
2. Trace at least two defining practices to a material, historical, or philosophical cause.
3. Show how major values affect more than one part of life.
4. Distinguish cultural ideals from ordinary behaviour.
5. Identify at least one tension between values, institutions, classes, regions, or generations.

Use [[Worksheets/012-W01 - Culture Coherence Worksheet|Culture Coherence Worksheet]] when the culture reads as a list of aesthetic traits.

### 7. Connect history to the present

1. Include only events that still affect current behaviour, identity, territory, institutions, or relationships.
2. Explain what each major event changed.
3. Link predecessor states, successor states, wars, migrations, reforms, disasters, and treaties when they matter.
4. Separate remembered history, official history, and established canon when they differ.

### 8. Explain armed force and security when relevant

1. Identify what the polity must defend or control.
2. Identify who supplies personnel, equipment, food, transport, and command.
3. Explain whether forces are permanent, seasonal, conscripted, professional, allied, corporate, or irregular.
4. State the constraints that limit mobilisation and endurance.
5. Connect military doctrine to terrain, institutions, industry, and political goals.

Do not derive military size from population alone.

### 9. Adapt the research baseline

1. Open [[Worksheets/012-W04 - CITADEL and SMOG Research Baseline Worksheet|CITADEL and SMOG Research Baseline Worksheet]] when historical comparison is useful.
2. Record the external baseline.
3. Record the VISCERIUM decision.
4. Record the cause of the departure.
5. Record the consequences created by the departure.
6. Record the final canon decision in the authoritative article or linked note.

Research provides constraints and questions. It does not become canon automatically.

### 10. Add story pressure

1. Identify one conflict visible to ordinary residents.
2. Identify one deeper conflict over authority, dependency, legitimacy, memory, or resources.
3. Identify who benefits from the current system.
4. Identify who pays its cost.
5. Identify one change that could destabilise the system.

Keep encyclopaedic facts in Lore. Follow [[Storyteller View SOP]] for practical story guidance.

## Check the result

Confirm these statements:

1. The polity has a clear identity, era, and geographic scope.
2. Its population, economy, culture, institutions, and conflicts do not contradict one another without explanation.
3. Important claims have an identified cause, source, or recorded assumption.
4. At least one meaningful internal pressure can produce change or story.
5. Optional detail has not become automatic work debt.

Run `cd Site && npm run doctor:vault` after structural changes.

## Stop condition

Stop at publication readiness when the article serves its public purpose.

Continue to reference readiness only when a current project requires deeper support.
