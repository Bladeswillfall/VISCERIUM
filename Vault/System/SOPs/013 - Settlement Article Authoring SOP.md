# Settlement Article Authoring SOP

> **Use this SOP when:** You create or revise a city, town, village, fort, port, mining camp, mobile community, or other settlement.
>
> **Result:** The article explains why the settlement exists, what sustains it, and what pressures shape daily life.
>
> **First action:** State the settlement's location, era, and job in one sentence.

Follow [[Documentation Writing Standard]] for operational wording.

## Purpose

Make settlements feel placed rather than decorative.

Connect location, water, food, movement, work, authority, security, culture, and change where those facts affect use.

## Before you start

1. Confirm that the settlement needs its own note.
2. Set the controlled era.
3. Link the containing region or polity.
4. Open the relevant map, route, river, coast, or neighbouring settlement notes.
5. Decide whether the article needs publication readiness or reference readiness.

## Procedure

### 1. Give the settlement a job

Write one sentence in this form:

> This settlement exists here because [geographic, logistical, political, industrial, defensive, ritual, or historical reason].

If no clear reason exists, move, reduce, combine, or remove the settlement.

### 2. Test the location

1. Identify the source of fresh water.
2. Identify the source of food.
3. Identify the routes that connect the settlement to producers, markets, allies, and frontiers.
4. Identify nearby junctions, chokepoints, anchorages, extraction sites, surplus zones, and hazards.
5. Identify the worst constraint on growth.

Use [[Worksheets/013-W01 - Settlement Location and Support Worksheet|Settlement Location and Support Worksheet]] when the location or scale is uncertain.

### 3. Set scale honestly

1. Choose a qualitative population band when no dependable estimate exists.
2. Record the assumptions behind any numerical population.
3. Test whether water, food, fuel, housing, sanitation, transport, labour, and security can support the stated scale.
4. Identify seasonal or emergency changes.
5. Record what happens when the limiting system fails.

Use [[Worksheets/013-W02 - CITADEL Population Estimate Worksheet|CITADEL Population Estimate Worksheet]] only when its pre-industrial assumptions apply.

Do not use that estimator unchanged for SMOG, NEARSIGHT, or ENTROPY.

### 4. Explain movement and access

1. Explain how residents and goods enter, cross, and leave the settlement.
2. Identify the main gate, road, dock, railhead, lift, tunnel, air route, or orbital connection as applicable.
3. Explain who controls access.
4. Identify tolls, inspections, hazards, seasonal closures, and informal routes when they matter.
5. Connect movement to trade, class, defence, and political authority.

### 5. Explain work and supply

1. Identify the work that justifies the settlement's continued existence.
2. Identify what it produces, processes, stores, protects, administers, or exchanges.
3. Identify essential imports.
4. Identify the owners or institutions that control important labour, land, tools, fuel, storage, or specialist knowledge.
5. Trace one major supply dependency to its source.

### 6. Explain authority and services

1. Identify formal authority.
2. Identify practical authority.
3. Explain how residents obtain justice, credit, information, shelter, treatment, education, worship, and protection where relevant.
4. Identify groups that receive unequal access.
5. State what the settlement expects from residents in return.

### 7. Show the physical layout

1. Place major districts according to terrain, movement, work, status, water, waste, and defence.
2. Explain why wealthy, dangerous, industrial, sacred, or marginal spaces occupy their locations.
3. Connect walls, harbour works, forts, roads, bridges, rail, and public buildings to the threats or systems they serve.
4. Record one visible historical scar or adaptation.
5. Use [[Atlas Authoring SOP]] when the layout needs a public map.

### 8. Show daily culture

1. Identify what residents notice as normal.
2. Identify one practice shaped by local work or geography.
3. Identify one status marker.
4. Identify one local fear, grievance, pride, or taboo.
5. Distinguish official identity from ordinary behaviour.

Do not duplicate the parent nation's culture without showing local variation.

### 9. Explain security and vulnerability

1. Identify the threats the settlement expects.
2. Identify which direction, route, district, or system receives the most protection.
3. Identify what is neglected.
4. Identify who responds to fire, attack, unrest, disease, shortage, Myrkild activity, or infrastructure failure.
5. Identify one vulnerability that cannot be solved without external help.

### 10. Add story pressure

1. Identify one immediate local problem.
2. Identify one conflict between residents, institutions, districts, or outsiders.
3. Identify one dependency that can be exploited or interrupted.
4. Identify one reason to arrive.
5. Identify one reason to leave.

Follow [[Storyteller View SOP]] for practical encounter and story guidance.

## Check the result

Confirm these statements:

1. The settlement has a clear reason to exist at its location.
2. Its scale agrees with its supporting systems or records the deliberate exception.
3. Its layout reflects movement, work, authority, status, history, and threat.
4. At least one dependency and one tension can produce consequences.
5. The article does not imply unsupported numerical precision.

Run `cd Site && npm run doctor:vault` after structural changes.

## Stop condition

Stop when a creator can place, describe, enter, use, and pressure the settlement without inventing its defining systems.
