# Political Character Authoring SOP

> **Use this SOP when:** You create or revise a character whose role affects an institution, faction, settlement, economy, military, or political conflict.
>
> **Result:** The character has credible leverage, personal stakes, predictable strengths, and a breaking point.
>
> **First action:** Identify the institution or power centre that gives the character influence.

Follow [[Documentation Writing Standard]] for operational wording.

## Purpose

Create political characters whose decisions arise from role, ambition, cognition, relationships, and contradiction.

Avoid characters who are important only because the article calls them important.

## Before you start

1. Confirm that the character needs an individual note.
2. Set the controlled era.
3. Link the institution, faction, office, territory, or resource that supports their influence.
4. Identify the current story or worldbuilding decision that the character must affect.
5. Open relevant relationship and predecessor notes.

## Procedure

### 1. Define institutional obligations

1. State what the character's office or power centre wants.
2. State what the character is expected to protect, increase, enforce, or prevent.
3. Identify the resources, territory, legitimacy, information, wealth, or force available to the role.
4. Identify who can remove, constrain, replace, or punish the character.
5. Separate the office's goal from the person's goal.

### 2. Define personal ambition

1. State what the character wants for themselves.
2. State why they want it.
3. Identify the cost they are prepared to impose on others.
4. Identify the cost they refuse to pay.
5. State where personal ambition conflicts with institutional duty.

### 3. Define cognitive architecture

1. Identify how the character collects and interprets information.
2. Identify how the character makes decisions.
3. Identify how the character evaluates risk.
4. Identify one cognitive strength.
5. Identify one predictable limitation.
6. State the class of mistake that follows from the limitation.

Do not use a trait only as decoration. Show how it changes decisions.

### 4. Define hidden contradiction

1. Identify a belief, desire, fear, loyalty, dependency, or past action that conflicts with the public role.
2. State what exposure would cost.
3. State how the character lies to themselves about it.
4. Identify the situation most likely to force it into the open.

### 5. Establish leverage

1. Identify what the character directly controls.
2. Identify what they influence through relationships.
3. Identify what they can credibly promise.
4. Identify what they can credibly threaten.
5. Identify one limit that prevents unlimited action.

Political importance must come from a named institution, resource, relationship, dependency, reputation, or capability.

### 6. Build relationships

1. Identify who the character protects, mentors, commands, serves, owes, or exploits.
2. Identify one public ally.
3. Identify one private or fragile alliance.
4. Identify one person they support but distrust.
5. Identify who distrusts them and why.
6. Use [[Relationship Authoring SOP]] for meaningful structured relationships.

### 7. Run the crisis test

1. Create one crisis that puts institutional duty, personal ambition, cognitive limitation, and hidden contradiction into conflict.
2. State what the character loses by serving the institution.
3. State what they betray by pursuing personal ambition.
4. State how their thinking makes the decision harder.
5. State what happens if the contradiction becomes public.
6. Choose the action the character is most likely to take.
7. Record the consequence that follows.

Use [[Worksheets/014-W01 - Political Character Conflict Worksheet|Political Character Conflict Worksheet]] when the layers do not yet collide.

### 8. Connect the character to change

1. Identify one policy, appointment, alliance, dispute, reform, war, or economic decision the character can affect.
2. Identify who benefits.
3. Identify who pays the cost.
4. Identify what resistance the action creates.
5. Identify how the consequence changes the character's next decision.

## Verification checklist

Use [[Checklists/014a - Political Character Publication Checklist|Political Character Publication Checklist]] after the article work is complete.

## Check the result

Confirm these statements:

1. The character's office has obligations distinct from personal ambition.
2. Their influence comes from concrete leverage.
3. Their way of thinking creates both an advantage and a predictable mistake.
4. A hidden contradiction threatens their public role.
5. One plausible crisis forces all four layers into conflict.

Run `cd Site && npm run doctor:vault` after structural changes.

## Stop condition

Stop when the character can make a consequential decision without the writer inventing a new motive, power source, or limitation.
