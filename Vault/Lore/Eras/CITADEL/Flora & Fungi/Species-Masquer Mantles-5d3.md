Strain pressure: Envy Silhouette: Immense shelf-like drapes growing from cliff faces, ruins, and canyon walls, layered in rippling fans that change colour with angle and season. What they really are: Vast mantle colonies that imitate neighbouring surfaces. From afar they can look like slate, pine-shadow, lichen, old masonry, or snow-stained rock. How they compete: Theft of identity. They hide real stone under false skin, steal light from cliff flora, lure nesting animals to the wrong ledges, and make rival growths visually irrelevant. That lines up well with Envy’s masks, skulking presence, and false perception. Behaviour: New growth often appears exactly where scouts thought there was a safe ledge or cave mouth. Use: Hunters cut mantle strips for camouflage screens and decoy blinds. Fear: Pathfinders hate them because they make landscapes lie.
