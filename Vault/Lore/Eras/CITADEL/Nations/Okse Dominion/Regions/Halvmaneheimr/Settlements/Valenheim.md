> Valenheim has three types of weather; cold and wet, cold and snowing, or just *really fuckin' cold*.
>
> — [[Frode Altoren]]

## The Black Crown
[[Lore/Eras/CITADEL/Nations/Okse Dominion/Regions/Halvmaneheimr/Settlements/Valenheim|Valenheim]] does not announce itself with towers. The road narrows between white slopes, pines thin beneath the wind and the sky compresses between the shoulders of the [[Halvmane Mountains]]. Then the basin opens and the south is sealed by black stone so long the eye mistakes it for geology. A goat-horn blown from [[Mestrvirki]] carries across the snow before the gate is visible; somewhere below the road, a taglharpa can sound close enough to touch.

Mestrvirki spans roughly thirty-five kilometres across the southern breach, biting into mountain at either end. Calling it a city wall is convenient and wrong; much crosses ravines and watch-country where no civilian street reaches. Valenheim clings where wall, road and the [[Issvatn river|Issvatn]] have been forced to agree.

From a distance the barrier is clean. Up close it is a bastard of different ages. Barracks sit over stores; stores cut into older galleries; families cook in chambers first meant for soldiers. Oil sweats beneath tarred awnings. Smoke leaks from arrow slits. Repair scaffolds lean across masonry claimed, depending on the mason and how much mead he has had, by the [[Vetruxi Kingdom]], older basin peoples, or nobody worth arguing about anymore.

The gates have accumulated the same mess. Marks of [[Vordr the Warden]] and [[Visi the Sage]] share stone with wolf teeth, finger-bones, black thread, carved oxen, children's ribbons and [[Myrkild]]-infected claws nailed upside-down. Priests call it superstition. Masons rarely remove it.

At [[Uxagrind]], a corpse cage hangs beneath both gods.

## Six Faces of the Same City
Valenheim is easier to remember by smell, incline and who is allowed to stand still than by any map. Petitioners, merchants, levy clerks, wounded soldiers, noble households and foreign envoys pass through the same gates, but they do not inhabit the same city. Imported cloth stays clean because somebody else washes it. Rope cuts a porter’s palms. Smiths carry burns. Soldiers carry old limps. Prisoners carry iron.

Then a ragged clerk produces the right [[Maelihus]] seal and everyone gets out of his fucking way.

### Hofn Brattrstein
At [[Hofn Brattrstein]], the day begins before the light. Pike and [[Elvormr]] are split on black boards while dockers shout berth numbers through fog. Blood runs pink between quay stones. [[Sigdfugl]] circle the roofs and wait for the gulls to miss something.

Yrsa Hrafnsdottir is usually shouting before sunrise. One winter morning a fish cart broke an axle across an oil berth as three horn-blasts announced a convoy. Men swore; the owner swore louder. [[Svartskjoldr]] came down in blackened armour beside sealed casks and fire-tubes, paid to imagine the entire fucking quay on fire. Yrsa kept gutting pike while everyone else dragged the cart aside.

The oil got its berth.

It always does.

Grain, salt, timber, livestock, ore and people share the same wet congestion. Free porters work beside [[Thralls]] and [[Ambátts]] marked by collars, brands or an overseer close enough to strike them. A dropped barrel enters the tally; a dislocated shoulder usually does not. Toll disputes make salt dear, seized barges provoke searches and foreign coin is cursed according to whichever border lord last fucked with it. Merchants keep three prices: peace, rumour and soldiers asking for wagons.

Over the gutting tables hang little pike-bone cages knotted with black wool and copper. Priests say they do nothing. Fishwives retie them before first frost anyway, because priests sleep farther from the river.

Warehouses crowd chandlers, brokers, brothels, cheap beds and [[Klofinn Kjolr|the Klofinn Kjolr]], its floor sanded against fish slime and blood. A dried Elvormr jaw hangs over the bar. The proprietor says the beast was longer than his boat. Every fisherman knows this is bullshit. Every fisherman has seen a larger one. The board lists pot, mead, hot water, bed-space and “damage to furniture”; only the last changes nightly.

[[Ulfrvaldar]] are easy to spot by old bite-scars and the habit of checking every doorway before sitting. Their wolves stay in the yard after what happened to Orri's hand. Ask three regulars what happened and you will get four answers, two of which involve Orri being drunk and one in which he deserved it.

> Move your fucking boots. The river's already had its share.
>
> — Yrsa Hrafnsdottir, to a man bleeding beside her stall

The smell of Brattrstein follows you uphill.

### Gamaltrappir
The [[Gamaltrappir]] climb through foundations older than the families living on them. One doorway opens at another man's roof. Cellars stop against masonry too expensive to remove. During thaw, water remembers streets that people have forgotten and comes up through the floor to prove it.

Three families may share one stove; lodging houses rent the same bed to different bodies by shift. Chamber pots are meant for the cesspits. They do not always reach them. Lice are too normal for conversation. Rats are not.

> DO NOT SHIT ABOVE THE THIRD STEP.
>
> — painted beside a runoff channel

Somebody crosses out *NOT* often enough that the ward clerk now repaints only the missing letters.

This closeness is useful until somebody goes wrong.

There is no single name for every Myrkild change. Gamaltrappir uses the practical one. Fever is ordinary; flesh knitting in the wrong shape is not. Nor are translucent growths, teeth falling loose without rot, or a wound refusing to behave like a wound. When suspicion falls on a room, neighbours listen through walls. Work stops before hunger does.

One thaw, a laundress kept her youngest boy in two shirts after something pale webbed beneath his shoulder. She said he had burned himself. The woman downstairs knew she was lying, and knew what an emptied household meant. For three days she brought soup and said nothing. On the fourth, the boy screamed hard enough for the whole stair to hear.

Valenheim remembers outbreaks better than the families inside them.

Poor households learn which symptoms wool can hide. Wealthy ones can afford private healers and better words for the same thing. Nobody calls concealment cowardice when it is their child.

Door-lintels carry wards layered like old handwriting—teeth, beads, red thread, carved bone. When somebody dies, some families press a soot-black thumb to the frame; others knot grey-blue cloth to [[Hrimbru]], where the [[Hnutverdir]] remember names the official rolls have misplaced.

Cross the river and the smell changes first.

### Fagrvatnhlid
[[Fagrvatnhlid]] rises toward [[Hofn Fagrvatn]], and almost everything unpleasant is removed before residents are expected to notice it. Courtyards are swept before dawn. Covered drains carry away what Gamaltrappir leaves in gutters. Even the arguments tend to happen behind better doors.

Most people experience Kónungsson Erlend Nautleif and Kónungsdóttir Signy Gunnvidottir through wax: harbour tallies, ration marks, labour summons and requisitions. The [[Maelihus]] can turn a household decision into grain release, quay-space or punishment before breakfast. The wrong seal on the right piece of parchment can ruin a merchant's week.

Old families boast that their names predate the oil yards. New *cask-lords* need only point at the lamps. At the [[Hvitgufulaug]] both soak in mineral heat while servants warm towels and carry imported wine upstairs where the bottle can be seen. Winter gardens survive behind high walls, fed with soil hauled uphill by people returning to rooms without gardens.

The rich are not immune to Valenheim. They are simply better at paying somebody else to shovel it.

When the wind turns south, even Fagrvatnhlid smells [[Svartsmidja]].

### Svartsmidja
Smoke thickens before the district appears. Armourers, foundries, oil yards and [[Svar Resonants]] crowd the roads feeding Mestrvirki. Hammer blows overlap until no forge keeps another's rhythm; quench troughs hiss, burners roar and soot settles into snow, hair and bread. Svartsmidja is warmer than most of Valenheim and correspondingly worse for the lungs.

Oil changed more than the lamps. Masters call steady winter light Oksmun ingenuity. Labourers call it “a longer day with a brighter fucking lamp.” Skilled free workers can demand higher wages. Thralls haul slag and fuel through the same yards, sometimes becoming indispensable to work they still have no right to refuse.

At shift-change, [[Hrolfs Blodpylsa]] does its best business. Smiths eat onion, oat-blackened sausage and whatever grey substance Hrolf insists has always been part of the recipe. He says anyone calling it sawdust is free to buy something dearer. Nobody has yet discovered what the dearer thing is.

The workshops keep Mestrvirki alive. [[Svartskjoldr]] arrive with scorched cast-bronze valves and split reinforced leather hose; [[Steinnbendir]] are called where foundations crack or collapsed galleries must be made safe before the bodies are counted. Before a new furnace is worked, smiths strike three blows against a cold anvil for Vordr. At [[Thrineisti Smidja]], Svar workers trap the first sparks beneath copper until the new moon. The master insists this is technical procedure.

His workers grin every time the bastard says it.

Follow the repaired armour and the road eventually reaches Virkivordr.

### Virkivordr
[[Virkivordr]] presses into Mestrvirki until neighbourhood and fortress become the same thing. Barracks stand above workshops, workshops above magazines, families beside armouries and signal towers. Children learn bell-codes before letters and drill with barrel-lid shields. Veterans sit outside cookshops criticising every company that passes, including those they once commanded.

Valenheim keeps only four [[Juggernauts]], which is enough. Children know their names. Adults pretend not to stare. Doors look smaller around them; horses dislike them; tavern benches suffer in their wake. When one crosses toward Uxagrind in full plate the street opens without an order, then closes again behind him as though embarrassed by the interruption.

Not every company passing those streets returns from a war anyone has declared. A burned watchtower, disputed pasture, seized toll-boat or dead cousin can keep two holds raiding for a season while both courts insist the peace stands. Valenheim experiences geopolitics as dearer salt, missing wool, requisitioned carts and names added to casualty boards. One dead son can be honour. Three can be a farm without enough hands to keep its lease.

Veterans remember who held the line. Widows remember who ordered them there.

The severe [[Raudvordr]] keep a soot-black shrine near Uxagrind, its ceiling hung with named spearheads from sentries killed at post. Their priests teach that vigilance is worship and mercy becomes vanity when it endangers those behind stone.

Outside, quartermasters repeatedly issue a notice forbidding soldiers to throw bones from the west parapet. It now specifies that chicken counts.

> VISI CAN JUDGE ME WHEN VORDR GETS ME HOME.
>
> — anonymous Virkivordr graffito

Beyond the gate, Valenheim becomes somebody else's problem.

### Sudrvellir
The [[Sudrvellir]] look peaceful from Mestrvirki: rye, barley, sheep, goats and thick-furred cattle spread over softer hills beneath the mountain ring. Up close they smell of dung, wet fleece and smoke. Farm families maintain stone refuges and livestock lanes because the wall protecting Valenheim does not automatically protect the people feeding it.

Black ribbons mark trees struck by unfamiliar blight. Iron nails pin strange claws to fence posts. Bowls of watered blood sometimes appear beside culverts after dark. Priests dismiss much of this as frightened nonsense. Farmers answer that priests sleep on the correct side of Uxagrind.

A malformed lamb presents a harder theology. Some are burned immediately. Others are watched because destroying half a flock whenever something is born wrong means starving correctly and piously in winter. One farmer is said to have kept a two-jawed calf alive for six weeks because his children liked it and it still drank milk. The story always ends badly. The ending changes depending on who tells it.

[[Ulfrvaldar]] work the rough country beyond with hunting packs bred to follow scents ordinary hounds will not. Farmers know them by their whistles, scars and the argument that starts whenever a valuable ewe disappears. The wolf-master blames a wild animal. The farmer blames his wolves. The ewe remains dead either way.

*Virkivarmr* is the Sudrvellir word for anyone giving brave advice from behind Mestrvirki. Priests dislike the expression, which has done wonders for its popularity.

## Stone Beneath Stone
The [[Okse Dominion]] did not find an empty valley. It inherited one already argued over by the dead.

Older riverworks survive beneath Gamaltrappir. Vetruxi foundations sit inside later walls. Streets kink around reasons nobody remembers. Cellars open into blocked passages; bridge piers disappear deeper beneath the Issvatn than anyone expected when they first tried repairing them. Valenheim has rebuilt itself for so long that architecture has become unreliable testimony.

When ordinary crews meet stone that refuses to behave, a [[Steinnbendir]] may be summoned. Masons admire the result and resent the speed. “Six months of honest labour,” goes one guild saying, “or one registered bastard before supper.” Nobody repeats it loudly near the [[Steinbokr]].

### The Scars People Still Name
Valenheim counts history by the winters that fucked something up badly enough to earn a name.

The [[Svartthidna]] is remembered for the smell: sudden warmth ruptured cesspits and sent old filth through forgotten drains. Wealth climbed uphill. The poor boiled what they could. The [[Saltvetr]] came when southern traffic failed and reserve houses opened under guard; laws written in that shortage still let the Maelihus seize private stores. Three taverns claim the first salt-riot tooth landed on their floor.

The [[Tannvetr]] taught Valenheim that the northern heights could kill as efficiently as the southern breach. Farmsteads vanished; refugees reached Uxagrind frost-black and terrified. Then the gate closed.

The official stone says the decision saved the city. Some families still refuse to pass beneath that inscription on Tannvetr's anniversary. They take the longer way around and call the dead by name.

The [[Uxagrindskjalfti]] left a different scar. Gatehouse stone began to hum, shifted less than a hand's breadth and killed seven men during repair. New inspection rites, engineering law and the first form of the Steinbokr followed. Whether the failure was Resonant, structural or both remains disputed, which has not stopped everyone from using it to prove whatever they believed already.

Children make all four disasters smaller enough to carry:

> Black water, white teeth,
> salt gone, stone sings.
> Stay by the fire,
> mind the bells,
> don't ask what winter brings.
>
> — Gamaltrappir skipping rhyme

## What Feeds the City
Valenheim's strength is described in stone. Its real vulnerability floats.

The Issvatn carries cargo between the capital and [[Vagrvik]], where southern trade transfers inland. Local rye, barley, dairy, livestock and fish feed much of the basin, but not nearly forty-seven thousand people through every winter. Salt matters because it keeps meat and fish edible after the river freezes. A missing salt cask is therefore not petty theft for long.

Mead belongs almost everywhere. Sloe and juniper spirits belong wherever cold, pain or poor judgement need assistance. Wine comes from elsewhere and travels uphill. In Fagrvatn a bottle is hospitality. In Brattrstein it is evidence somebody has either done very well or something deeply suspicious.

### The Road North
Some mornings Brattrstein goes quieter before the bells because somebody hears chains.

Prisoner columns gather near [[Hrimbru]] and leave for [[Lore/Eras/CITADEL/Nations/Okse Dominion/Regions/Halvmaneheimr/Settlements/Rauthrbak Min|Rauthrbak Min]] linked wrist-to-wrist or neck-to-neck. Officials call them *labour transfers*. Mine clerks prefer *northern service*. Gamaltrappir calls them *redbacks*. Families call whatever name is being taken from them.

Grey-blue ribbons appear on Hrimbru before a column leaves. The Guard cuts them down every few months. They return before the next chain does.

Children sing:

> Redback, redback, count the chain.
> One comes home and nine remain.

Adults tell them to shut up when prisoners can hear. The children mostly remember when an adult is looking.

### What Keeps the Hearths Burning
Valenheim eats fuel like a living thing. Timber and charcoal keep ordinary hearths going; [[Okse oil]] feeds lamps, specialised furnaces, wall stores and houses wealthy enough to value steady light over cost. Children gather broken packing wood. Oil disappears from yards a cup at a time. In the coldest weeks, the fine for stealing heat can be less frightening than behaving yourself.

A stolen cup of lamp oil is a *warm finger*. A stolen barrel is “something you never heard about.”

> HEARTH ALLOCATION FOLLOWS SERVICE, HOUSEHOLD AND NECESSITY, IN THAT ORDER.
>
> — Maelihus emergency proclamation

Nobody misses which word comes first.

## Behind the Wall
Mestrvirki is protection, workplace, neighbourhood, shrine, prison wall and threat depending on where a person is standing. Its inhabited stretches contain granaries, smithies, cisterns, signal rooms, armouries and families who have lived inside military stone for generations. The [[Valenheim City Guard]] is only one machine among garrisons, household troops, levies, engineers and the wider army.

Justice lives there too. Stocks stand near markets, flogging rings in gate courts and cages on towers facing incoming roads. The Dominion prefers punishment that can educate a crowd.

[[Marth Gamli]], executioner at Uxagrind, buys onions from the same Sudrvellir stall every market-day and complains if they are bruised. On punishment mornings he buys them first, before the crowd thickens. The onion seller does not move her stall. Neither does the man selling honeyed buns two doors down. Once, while a thief screamed through the loss of two fingers, the bun seller and Marth argued over whether last year's honey had been better.

The thief survived. So did the argument.

Other warnings are less official. Deep scratches remain on a northern tower because nobody has proved what made them. Iron grates close tunnels too narrow for siege parties. Children are taught never to investigate crying outside the refuge line after dark. Seventeen explanations circulate for why nobody should whistle into a wall culvert after sunset. Six contradict one another.

Nobody whistles.

CITADEL Valenheim does not understand the [[Myrkild]] as one coherent thing. It does not need the correct explanation every time. It needs enough people to hear the bell and close the fucking door.

## Beneath the Two Eyes
Valenheim claims to live beneath two principles. [[Vordr]] is vigilance, protection, ambition and war; [[Visi]] justice, prudence, empathy and memory. Oksmun tradition calls them siblings of balance, giving Valenheim two languages for every decision it already wanted to make.

Close Uxagrind and Vordr protected the many. Ask about those outside and Visi starts being inconvenient. Register a Resonant child and Vordr calls it vigilance. Take that child from a terrified household and Visi asks what the law was supposed to protect.

Valenheim is better at doctrine than balance.

The [[Tviaugahof]] is not a cathedral but a weather-beaten compound of standing stones, smoke-dark timber, oath-rings, sacrificial basins and an old conifer burdened with ribbons, bone, teeth, coins and petitions sealed against rain with fat. Vordr's altar receives iron, horn, broken weapons and blood. Visi's receives river stones, written names, cords and belongings of the dead. The [[Tviaugaverdir]] insist they should remain equal.

> VISI SEES YOU WANKING.
>
> — graffito scrubbed from the rear wall twice each winter

Vordr's altar is larger.

### The Ox-Father
[[Hof Uxifadir]] honours [[Torrad Uxifadir]] as Ox-Father, Kóngr Eilífr and root of Vetruxi inheritance—not a third eye beside the gods, officially. The distinction softens near a fire. [[Hornbundnir]] veterans, engineers and traditionalists maintain ancestral names and old banners; some pray to Torrad whenever nobody is asking the theological question too precisely.

At first-freeze, households gather to carry fire from the Hof and relight winter lamps. From the front steps it looks like one people sharing one flame. Then rank starts sorting the queue. Noble households stand where they can be seen. Soldiers receive precedence. Thralls carry fuel and clean blood from the sacrificial stone without standing among the households the rite claims to bind together.

A child once asked why Visi did not mind. His mother covered his mouth before the priest could answer.

### Little Wards Against a Large World
The Hof explains doctrine. Valenheim prefers things that can be scratched, knotted, burned or nailed down.

Runes are recut after children survive fever. River-workers carry drilled stones. Tavernkeepers hide teeth above doors. A Myrkild claw has survived three repaintings over the Klofinn Kjolr's back entrance because nobody is sufficiently convinced it does nothing to be the cunt who removes it.

At [[Galdrgata]], three adjacent sellers offer incompatible wards against the same curse and each can explain why the other two are trying to get customers killed. The [[Raudvordr]] survive because soldiers admire them; the Hnutverdir because cutting mourning ribbons creates more trouble than leaving them; the Hornbundnir because they have patrons. Smaller devotions gather around widows, veterans, shrine-keepers and [[Resonance|Resonants]] whenever neighbours decide somebody has started listening.

Darker things travel south from Rauthrbak Min. Sevenfold marks of the [[Heptad Synod]] appear on confiscated objects. A few northern-road cellars host private suppers for officials and wealthy men who have learned to call suffering revelation when they are not the ones bleeding.

## Power That Must Be Counted
The Okse admire [[Muld|Muld Resonants]] and fear them for the same reason: they are useful enough to build around and dangerous enough to count.

Their inheritance survives in structures ordinary engineers can maintain but cannot confidently reproduce. The [[Hljodbogi]] bridge meets without visible mortar and rings when floodwater reaches its third pier. The buried [[Vokugrunnr]] barely moved during the Svartthidna while newer masonry cracked around it. Masons leave offerings in its inspection niche despite orders not to obstruct survey marks.

The [[Steinbokr]] records practitioners, apprenticeships and authorised works. Unregistered affinity is a capital offence. Officially this is *compulsory affinity registration*. Gamaltrappir calls it “putting your child in the Jarl's pocket.”

Sometimes the first sign is dramatic. Sometimes a spoon slides sideways across a table.

A family sees it. The child sees them see it. For a moment nobody moves.

Then the household must decide whether the gift belongs to the child, the family or Valenheim.

A noble can call registration patronage and hire a tutor. A poor household may watch the same talent become a posting decided behind a desk. Both children will be praised for serving the Dominion. Forged papers exist. So do clerks willing to lose real ones for the right price.

Either can put somebody on the north road.

## The City Oksmun Actually Live In
Most Valenheimers are not nobles, Resonants or heroes. They are cold, busy and late for something.

They break ice from buckets before dawn, dry socks beside smoky fires, lose wages to fines, teeth to age and fingers to frost. They know which bell means trouble and which horn is only livestock. They know where the piss freezes first on Gamaltrappir steps and which Brattrstein seller waters her mead. They know the Blalaug's towel fee is robbery and pay it anyway.

At the [[Blalaug]], dockworkers soak beside smiths and off-duty guards beneath beams furry with steam. The water is never as clean as advertised. A cup of [[syrmjolk]] costs less than the towel; mead costs more; the juniper spirit behind the counter is cheap enough to make both seem reasonable. Two alleys away, the [[Niu Krokar]] rents “discreet” rooms beneath a sign visible from Hrimbru and has survived three morality campaigns by paying fines faster than magistrates invent them.

People fall in love above filthy water. Children race sledges past flogging posts. A sex worker nurses a neighbour's child; a vicious gate guard carries onions home to his father. A Thrall may know more about a warehouse crane than the free man holding its ledger and still laugh when the overseer's hat falls into the Issvatn.

Yrsa drinks at the Klofinn Kjolr after the quay closes. Hrolf sometimes arrives from Svartsmidja with sausages nobody asked for. If Marth Gamli appears with onions, somebody asks whether he has been working. He tells them to mind their own fucking business.

Valenheimers use *monster* easily for things with too many joints or unfamiliar teeth. The word becomes harder around people they know. A mother hiding a changed child may be cursed if neighbours die. A captain who shuts Uxagrind on refugees may be carved in stone for saving everyone behind him. A sentence to Rauthrbak becomes *service* when a clerk has good handwriting.

The laws are usually clearer than the people.

A man can watch a thief lose fingers in the morning, haggle over onions at noon and be thrown out of the Klofinn Kjolr by night for insisting an Elvormr swallowed his uncle. Nobody finds this remarkable. Cheap kitchens serve rye, hard cheese, smoked fish and blood sausage; in Fagrvatn the plate arrives arranged, in Gamaltrappir in a bowl with instructions to stop fucking looking at it.

Somebody paints a shutter red because winter has been grey too long. Somebody else ties three wards to it because red without protection invites envy from anything with eyes. Somebody downstairs bangs on the ceiling because they are trying to sleep.

Human warmth exists everywhere.

Institutional comfort does not.

## What the Wall Leaves Behind
Ask five Valenheimers what keeps the city alive and they will insult one another before reaching six answers. A priest says vigilance. A merchant says the river. A smith says skilled hands. A noble says order. A farmer says food. A prisoner on the northern road is unlikely to be asked.

They are all partly right. Mountains narrow approach; Mestrvirki closes the basin; Issvatn moves freight; [[Vagrvik]] supplies what local land cannot; salt preserves winter food; fuel keeps hearths lit; engineers keep stone standing; soldiers close gates. Thralls and prisoners absorb costs free citizens prefer not to count.

None of it is clean. The same river carries grain, prayer ribbons, fish blood and sewage. The wall stops Myrkild and hangs cages beneath Vordr's runes. The sacred hearth promises communal survival while deciding who may stand nearest the flame. Valenheim praises balance and builds ever more confidently in the Warden's image.

By dusk the fish gutters freeze. Forge-smoke thins into the falling dark and lamps appear one by one across Fagrvatnhlid. Somewhere inside Mestrvirki a watch bell sounds once, then another answers farther along the black stone. The north road disappears beneath the mountains.

On Hrimbru, beneath a notice forbidding unauthorised petitions, somebody ties another grey-blue ribbon for Visi.

By morning the Guard may cut it down.

By evening there will be another.

## Storyteller View

> [!note] Storyteller principle
> Treat Valenheim as a lived capital whose systems are always visible at street level. Comfort, brutality, superstition and ordinary affection should overlap rather than take turns.

## Entering Valenheim
Give the visitor one dominant image first: Mestrvirki mistaken for geology, then the details that ruin its cleanliness—repair scaffolds, household smoke, cages, charms and people living inside military stone.

## Move Through Status
The same city changes with rank. At Brattrstein, blood freezes in gutters. At Fagrvatnhlid, servants remove filth before residents see it. Show that change spatially instead of explaining inequality from above.

Remember that capital traffic complicates local status. A ragged petitioner may carry a Jarl's seal; a foreign envoy may be rich and temporarily powerless; a clerk from the [[Maelihus]] can ruin a merchant's week with ink. Rank matters, but so do jurisdiction, paperwork and whose problem has become the state's problem.

## Use Named Places
Anchor scenes to recognisable micro-locations: [[Klofinn Kjolr]], [[Hrimbru]], [[Blalaug]], [[Niu Krokar]], [[Tviaugahof]] or [[Hljodbogi]]. Reuse them until characters develop opinions about them. Let the native name stand without translating it every time; the city should not sound as though it is naming itself for an outside reader.

## Let Belief Accumulate
Runes, ribbons, teeth, soot-marks and bone charms should be ordinary visual language. Formal doctrine belongs at the Hof; survival belief belongs everywhere else. Contradictory practices are a feature, not an error.

## The Northern Road
A Rauthrbak procession can remain background until somebody recognises a face in it. The horror is strongest when the baker continues serving customers while chains pass outside.

## Myrkild Without Combat
Use bell codes, burnt carcasses, locked grates, sudden gate closures, changed livestock, suspicious symptoms and inherited taboos. A Myrkild does not have to appear for Myrkild pressure to dominate a scene.

Keep the boundary between person and monster uncertain. The horror may be literal infection, a loved one hiding a change, or a perfectly healthy official making an appalling decision because they believe the alternative is worse. Do not let supernatural corruption become an excuse that absolves human cruelty.

## Competing Valenheims
Different people should believe different things about why the city survives and what it is becoming. Soldiers may credit the wall, merchants the river, engineers the works, traditionalists Torrad, labourers their own backs. Treat these as sincere partial truths rather than a single authorial answer. Ideology should emerge from material interests, history and lived experience.

## Resonance as Infrastructure
The Hljodbogi and Vokugrunnr are useful because they make Muld legacy tangible. A character can cross, inspect, worship, damage or exploit them without receiving a lecture on Resonance.

Do the same with professions. A [[Svartskjoldr]] should arrive smelling of oil and worrying about sparks; an [[Ulfrvaldr]] may be recognised by old bite scars before the wolves appear; a [[Steinnbendir]] is somebody a mason can admire, resent and still desperately need. Use the names first. Let behaviour teach the reader what they mean.

## Keep Voices Messy
Mix official proclamations, rude graffiti, children's rhymes, market complaints, dirty jokes, prices, boring rules and things nobody can agree on. Profanity should belong to speakers and situations, not be sprinkled mechanically. Let stupid concerns interrupt profound ones.

A scene becomes more Valenheim when the funeral has to make room for a dung cart, the priest loses an argument with a fishwife, or somebody complains about towel charges during a discussion of divine balance.

## End on an Image
When possible, stop before explaining the theme. A ribbon retied after officials cut it down often says more than another paragraph about resistance, memory or mercy.
