The **Circle of Trades** brings together twenty-one of the [[Lore/Eras/CITADEL/Nations/Republic of Askalia/Republic of Askalia|Republic of Askalia]]'s most prominent guilds. Its Guildmasters control much of the organisation behind the nation's crafts, trade, labour and commercial wealth.

The Circle was established to represent its members, share knowledge and regulate trades which had previously been organised by individuals or scattered workshops. Some guilds maintain standards of work. Others control access to an occupation, coordinate major commissions or bargain on behalf of workers.

In Askalia, these are consequential powers.

## Origins and the death of the crown

Six Guildmasters are named in surviving accounts of the Circle's founding: **Balthasar Rolland**, Banking; **Fainnear Morgan**, Monster Hunting; **Iomhair Grayriver**, Forgemasters; **Liliana Dusksong**, Carpenters; **Rania Liafiel**, Stonemasons; and **Talan Umber**, Farmers.

Their guilds became the foundation of the Platinum Eye. Membership grew as other professions organised themselves and sought recognition within the Circle.

During the final years of the Kingdom of Askalia, pressure against [[Lore/Eras/CITADEL/Characters/Briar Remonnet|King Briar Remonnet]] mounted among the guilds and members of the [[Lore/Eras/CITADEL/Nations/Republic of Askalia/Organisations/Senate - Askalia|Senate]]. Members of the Circle participated in the coup that ended with the king's assassination.

The Circle survived the monarchy as an entirely private organisation. Its separation from government has not prevented Guildmasters from influencing senators through private agreements, bribery, blackmail and control of major economic interests.

## Seats and succession

Most Guildmasters are elected by members of their own guild. Election grants them a seat within the Circle.

The **Platinum Eye** follows different rules. Its six seats are legacy positions, held by successors chosen personally by the previous seat-holders.

The Grand Overseer historically exercised superior authority over other Guildmasters, encouraging cooperation and loyalty among the guilds. The position was customarily granted by the nation's ruler. No new Grand Overseer has been chosen since Remonnet's assassination.

## The Platinum Eye

### Banking Guild

One of Askalia's oldest guilds, the Banking Guild began by safeguarding the wealth of rulers and nobles. Its members kept detailed records of deposits, withdrawals and the estimated worth of the assets under their protection.

The guild remains closely associated with the management of significant fortunes.

### Monster Hunting Guild

Formed by guardsmen, hunters and soldiers, this guild deals with dangerous beasts and infected creatures. Its members capture, study, destroy or relocate threats where possible.

It also allocates Juggernauts to important locations and oversees their equipment and training.

### Forgemasters Guild

The Forgemasters are an exclusive association of skilled blacksmiths. Membership brings status to an individual craftsperson as well as business to their workshop, and the guild is protective of the standards expected of its members.

### Carpenters Guild

The Carpenters Guild standardises woodworking methods and preserves the techniques needed to make tools, furniture, buildings and other wooden goods.

### Stonemasons Guild

Stonemasons formed their guild for much the same reasons as the Carpenters: to preserve expertise, establish methods and pass on knowledge of their craft.

### Farmers Guild

Farmers formed a guild to give their profession a collective voice in decisions they had struggled to influence individually.

## The Golden Circle

### Alchemists Guild

The Alchemists Guild includes practitioners of alchemical medicine and related study. Its members work as local healers and prepare concoctions intended to treat ailments of the mind and body.

### Tailors Guild

The Tailors Guild ranks its craftspeople by skill, distinguishing makers of ordinary clothing from those able to produce elaborate garments of silk, gems and worked metal for wealthy patrons.

### Guardsmans Guild

Founded to regulate the loyalty and employment of professional guards, the Guardsmans Guild draws heavily upon former military personnel.

Those who refuse or fail to join may not lawfully earn money performing many forms of private security work, including acting as bodyguards, gatekeepers or peacekeepers. This control over employment makes the guild influential well beyond its own membership.

### Shipwrights Guild

Askalian shipwrights first joined forces to fulfil large orders from ambitious nobles, pooling labour, timber and other resources across separate workshops.

The arrangement allowed parts of a fleet to be constructed in different places before ships were assembled or brought together for their intended use.

## The Silver Circle

### Glassworkers Guild

Askalia's glasshouses were once scattered across the country's woodland areas, with kilns producing everything from common vessels to delicate chalices and windows.

The Glassworkers Guild emerged to share techniques and improve the standards of a trade whose workmanship varied considerably between workshops.

### Tanners Guild

Hunters and Pathfinders sometimes return with the bodies of creatures unfamiliar to the tanners expected to process them.

The Tanners Guild was formed partly to preserve and distribute knowledge of unusual hides, skins and other materials. Sharing methods reduces waste and makes an uncertain commission less costly.

### Hunters Guild

Hunting in unsettled country can bring people close to Demons and other dangerous creatures. Hunters formed a guild to share information about these risks and to record sightings.

Experience within the Hunters Guild can also provide a route towards work with the Pathfinders.

### Miners Guild

The Miners Guild began by consolidating competing mining interests and their wealth. Over time it also became an organisation through which miners improved their pay and working conditions.

### Fletchers Guild

Poorly made arrows caused failures on the battlefield, prompting the founding of the Fletchers Guild during the reign of Askalia's previous king.

The guild seeks consistent standards in the manufacture of arrows and related equipment.

## The Copper Circle

### Brewers Guild

The Brewers Guild was formed in response to deaths caused by badly prepared alcohol.

By spreading better methods, it helped reduce poisoning and improve the quality of ordinary drink. Some of its more successful members now experiment with expensive recipes aimed at noble households.

### Whalers Guild

Whalers pursue enormous prey with spears, nets, hooks and chains, hauling their catch into port.

Their guild is highly competitive, with prestige attached to the size and value of a kill. Unlike other associations, members are less inclined to share the techniques by which they gain an advantage.

### Fishermans Guild

The Fishermans Guild coordinates fishing routes, working schedules and access to particular waters, attempting to meet demand without exhausting the fish stocks on which its members depend.

Pooling practical knowledge also improves the efficiency of its crews.

### Pathfinders Guild

The guild developed to prevent Pathfinders from repeatedly surveying the same routes without sharing their findings.

By gathering maps, notes and surveys from its members, the organisation builds more complete records of distant territories and the dangers along their approaches.

### Historykeepers Guild

Historykeepers were once employed chiefly by nobles to record family histories and notable deeds. Conflicting accounts and overlapping records led to the creation of a guild responsible for preserving and comparing such histories.

Its members maintain a large bibliotheca containing written records and tapestries.

### Porters Guild

The Porters Guild began as an association of workers in [[Lore/Eras/CITADEL/Nations/Republic of Askalia/Kings Cliff|King's Cliff]] demanding better wages.

It subsequently spread to other cities and secured a seat within the Circle. Its origins as a workers' union remain distinct from those of guilds founded by wealthy merchants or established craftspeople.

## A common interest?

The Circle gathers professions that depend upon one another, but their needs are not always compatible. The Banking Guild safeguards wealth, the Porters bargain over wages, the Guardsmen regulate paid protection and the Miners seek safer work. Agreement among Guildmasters need not mean agreement among the people they represent.

Their collective influence has nonetheless survived the removal of the king who once stood above them.

## Storyteller View

### Recognisable presence

Guild seals and rank, arguments over the quality of work, disputes about who may practise a trade, and the influence of wealthy Guildmasters are familiar features of Askalian commercial life.

### Current agenda

The Circle's members seek to protect their trades, established privileges and representation. Individual guilds may disagree sharply over wages, access to work or the distribution of large commissions.

### Preferred methods

Trade regulation, collective bargaining, shared professional knowledge and private influence over the Senate. The Circle's history also includes direct participation in a royal assassination.

### Resources and limits

Twenty-one established guilds offer wealth, specialist labour and organisational reach. The guilds differ in size, purpose and interests; their cooperation cannot be assumed.

### Internal tensions

Hereditary Platinum Eye seats sit above elected Guildmasters. A former royal Grand Overseer's position remains unfilled, and the Circle includes both employers' associations and guilds with roots in workers' demands.

### Consequences of involvement

Membership may give a craftsperson access to work, training and collective support. Opposition to a powerful guild can jeopardise employment or business. A political favour from the Circle may carry commercial obligations.
