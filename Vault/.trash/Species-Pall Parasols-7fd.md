Strain pressure: Sloth Silhouette: Colossal black-grey caps rising from marshes and hollows like drowned umbrellas, their undersides fringed with hanging tendon-veils. What they really are: Vast colonial cap-growths, not true mushrooms in the mundane sense. The “stem” is a bundle of wet fibrous cords wrapped around a gelatinous load-bearing core. How they compete: They create their own dim biome. Under a Pall Parasol, light drops, air stills, and spores thicken. Rival growths don’t get burned out or strangled fast; they just get smothered into slow failure. That fits Sloth’s unstable, half-formed, sagging physical logic. Behaviour: The caps subtly deform over months, sagging into one another until whole marshes become a roofed duskland. Use: Dried veil-fibre can be turned into excellent filter cloth and rot-resistant field wraps. Fear: Sleeping beneath one for too long leaves people waking sluggish, confused, and unwilling to move. Some never really leave.
