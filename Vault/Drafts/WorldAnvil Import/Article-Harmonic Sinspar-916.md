> Do not mistake its polish for purity. The cleaner the Sinspar, the fouler the pit from which it was first taken.
> 
> — Maveth Orun, chamberwright of ______

  Harmonic Sinspar is no noble crystal wrested from mountain roots, nor some celestial gemstone gifted to the learned. It is a biocrystalline excrescence - an ugly elegance born of Myrkild feeding, secretion, and waste. Where the Myrkild strip biomass from flesh, root, and carrion alike, they consume what is useful and reject the silicates, salts, trace metals, calcified residue, and other inorganic burden they cannot meaningfully assimilate. Given time, pressure, and prolonged exposure to their warped bio-harmonics, that rejected matter hardens into dense nodules and eventually crystallises into what scholars, artificers, and [Resonants](/w/viscerium/a/resonance-article) alike have come to know as Harmonic Sinspar.   Though commonly associated with Rifts, the crystal is not born from the opening itself. A rift is merely a wound in the landscape through which Myrkild traffic passes in abundance. Sinspar forms there for the same reason it forms in feeding pits, nest-chambers, tunnel mouths, and corpse-heaps - such places accumulate immense quantities of processed refuse, secretions, and residue under constant Myrkild presence.  

## Formation

In its earliest state, Harmonic Sinspar begins as a foul accretion: mineral grit bound in resinous biological slurry, clotted through with translucent membranes, dead fibres, and hardened secretions. These first growths are rarely beautiful. They resemble tumour-stones, diseased geodes, or great mineral scabs budding from tunnel walls, carcass beds, and the sheathed interiors of old feeding chambers.   As more waste is expelled and layered atop the first deposits, the mass thickens. Myrkild secretions bind the rejected matter together, while the species’ ever-present bio-harmonics impose a crude order upon the forming lattice. The result is neither wholly geological nor wholly biological. It is something between the two - a crystal forced into shape by appetite, pressure, and an alien harmonic logic.   In mature deposits, the outer biological rind often dries, flakes, or rots away, revealing the sharper crystalline body within. It is these refined inner masses that are harvested, cut, and polished for use by mankind.  

## Appearance

Raw Harmonic Sinspar is seldom pleasant to behold. Unrefined deposits often bear dark seams, cloudy inclusions, mineral bruising, and vestiges of the foul material from which they emerged. Some masses appear waxen and translucent like old fat stretched thin over bone, while others are black-veined, wet-looking, and coarse as if grown from compressed ash and marrow.   When cut and polished, however, Sinspar takes on a colder majesty. Its planes can be brought to sharp edges and concave surfaces with surprising precision, and many artisans claim that such geometry improves the crystal’s appetite for excess Resonance. Fine pieces often show a muted inner lustre rather than the radiant beauty of Tideglass or the iridescent grandeur of [Galdyr / Galdrvyr](/w/viscerium/a/galdyr-2F-galdrvyr-material). Harmonic Sinspar does not seem to shine so much as drink the light around it.  

## Harmonic Behaviour

The defining property of Harmonic Sinspar is not that it sings with [[Drafts/WorldAnvil Import/Law-Resonance-433]], but that it _deadens_ it.   Where other materials may conduct, amplify, shape, or answer harmonic force, Harmonic Sinspar disrupts coherence. Resonance passing into the crystal finds no elegant path through which to travel. Instead, the ordered vibration is broken apart into countless dissonant micro-oscillations and dispersed through the body of the stone. To the eye, this may appear as a faint dimming, a dull internal pulse, or a brief swallowing of nearby light. To the ear, it is often accompanied by a low, suffocating hum or a dry crackling murmur like pressure moving through old ice.   This property makes Sinspar invaluable wherever Resonance must be contained, reduced, stabilised, or bled away. It is for this reason that polished Harmonic Sinspar is so often set into resonance chambers, attunement dais, reliquary housings, prison architecture, and the ritual spaces in which dangerous harmonic discharge is expected.   Yet the crystal is not limitless. A deposit pushed beyond its saturation threshold may fracture violently, shed razor shards, or release its stored instability as a concussive hum, making the extraction and use of raw masses an undertaking of no small danger.  

## Species-born Variance

Laymen often claim that differing strains produce wholly different forms of Harmonic Sinspar. This is only partly true. In truth, the gross morphology of Sinspar is more strongly shaped by species than strain. Species governs the secretion profile, feeding rhythm, and harmonic character imposed upon the growing crystal. Strain more often determines purity, density, saturation capacity, and failure behaviour.  

### Gluttony

Gluttony-born Sinspar most often forms as pitted geodes, maw-like nodules, and cavernous sink-crystals with dark hollow throats. These masses possess a deep appetite for incoming Resonance and can absorb an impressive volume before reaching saturation, yet they are notoriously unstable once overfed. Many chamberwrights prize them for crude sinks and sacrificial dampening structures, but few trust them in places where failure would be catastrophic.  

### Envy

Envy-born Sinspar tends toward mirrored plates, mask-like facets, and polished surfaces broken by eye-like inclusions. Its dampening is less brute in nature and more deceptive in effect. Resonant readings taken through Envy crystal are often muddled by false echoes, displaced frequencies, or misleading reflections in the harmonic field. Such pieces are highly valued by spies, occult scholars, and those who would rather hide a resonance signature than simply crush it.  

### Sloth

Sloth-born Sinspar grows in drooping shelves, waxy curtains, rope-veined masses, and thick translucent folds that seem half-finished even in maturity. Of all known forms, Sloth crystal is among the most oppressive in its effect. It does not so much break Resonance as smother it. Rooms lined with properly set Sloth Sinspar feel heavy, muted, and airless to many Resonants, as though every note they might utter is being swallowed before it can fully live.  

### Wrath

Wrath-born Sinspar is violent in form and behaviour alike. It grows as jagged fans, blade-clusters, splintered crowns, and fracture-knots jutting from walls and carcass beds like mineralised rage. When struck by harmonic force, it tends to shear that force apart abruptly and with little grace. Such deposits are dangerous to work, but highly prized where sudden and forceful dampening is desired, particularly in military fortifications, trapworks, and execution sites.  

### Lust

Lust-born Sinspar most often appears as ribboned crystal growths, fleshy-veined sheets, and smooth, curving forms with pearl-like inclusions. Its dampening quality is unusually conductive along surfaces, allowing Resonance to be drawn across a length of material before being bled away. For this reason, Lust crystal is favoured in certain binding instruments, ritual harnesses, and invasive medical apparatus where controlled contact matters more than brute suppression.  

### Pride

Pride-born Sinspar grows in ordered crowns, radial thorn-fans, needle lattices, and severe, elegant geometries. Among all varieties it is perhaps the most coveted by artificers and master Resonants, for its suppression is often the most selective and precise. Pride crystal can be cut and tuned to filter, narrow, and quiet particular harmonic expressions whilst allowing others to pass with less interference. Such work is costly, exacting, and often reserved for the powerful.  

### Greed

Greed-born Sinspar forms as heavy prismatic masses with dense metallic veining, gilded seams, and compressed angular growth. Slow to saturate and slower still to yield what it has taken in, Greed crystal is favoured for vaults, reliquaries, treasury wards, and other long-term containment uses. It is also infamous for drawing the covetous, for even unworked masses can appear strangely valuable to the unwise eye.

 

## Harvesting and Refinement

No sane miner seeks Harmonic Sinspar in the manner one seeks silver, salt, or marble. It is harvested from old Myrkild sites - from tunnel linings, collapsed brood chambers, excised masses, carcass vaults, and long-abandoned feeding grounds where the biological matter has rotted away enough to expose the crystal beneath.   The work is loathed. Raw deposits may still carry infectious residue, reactive secretions, or lingering suppressive fields that leave Resonants nauseous, dull-headed, and half-deaf to their own harmonic instincts. Worse still, immature masses sometimes hum in the presence of living flesh or crack without warning when disturbed.   Thus, refinement is as much purification as craftsmanship. The biological rind is stripped away, the mass cut down to stable core, and the crystal shaped into deliberate planes, channels, bowls, or concavities according to purpose. Many artisans refuse to work it at all, leaving the trade to prison labourers, condemned guilds, battlefield scavengers, and a small fraternity of specialist chamberwrights.

> 
> — Anonymous mason's saying

 

## Use in Attunement and Craft

Though foul in origin, Harmonic Sinspar has become indispensable in the higher arts of Resonant craft. It is most famously employed in the making of dais, pillars, and chamber architecture used during [Attunement|url], where excess harmonic discharge must be contained lest it shatter stone, deafen witnesses, or kill the practitioner outright.   Beyond such ritual spaces, Sinspar is also set into warding floors, reliquary casings, transport vaults, surgical tools, fetters, masks, and select pieces of armour intended to blunt hostile Resonance. Some smiths in later eras experiment with embedding narrow veins of Sinspar within other materials, creating composite tools that can channel one effect whilst suppressing another. Such techniques remain controversial, not least because many insist no clean work can ever come from so unclean a source.  

## Historical / Cultural Influence

Across much of history, Harmonic Sinspar has occupied a strange and uncomfortable place. It is too useful to abandon, too foul in origin to celebrate openly, and too closely tied to the Myrkild to ever be wholly trusted. The wealthy commission it in secret. Priests bless over it. Scholars dissect it. Soldiers pray it will hold when a Resonant siege begins. Few speak fondly of it.   Among some cultures, the material is considered ritually unclean and must be wrapped, etched, smoked, or consecrated before it can be brought into sacred or civic spaces. Others see in it a grim triumph - proof that even the waste of the enemy may be bent into human service. Certain frontier peoples even wear tiny slivers of Sinspar as ward-charms, believing that a little deadened song at the throat may turn aside hex, terror, or possession.   Naturally, a black market thrives around the material. False Sinspar, dyed glass, dead quartz, and mineral composites are all passed off as the true thing to the gullible and desperate. Most imitations fail their buyers at the worst possible moment.  

## Misconceptions

The first and most persistent falsehood is that Harmonic Sinspar is formed by the rift itself. This is untrue. The opening in the land is only a site of passage. The crystal forms because the Myrkild feed, process, reject, and accumulate there in great number.   The second is that Harmonic Sinspar and [Tideglass|url] are kindred materials. They are not. Tideglass bears the uncanny elegance of an environmental wonder. Harmonic Sinspar is an excrescence of alien processing made useful through human desperation and ingenuity. One is admired. The other is tolerated, feared, and employed.   The third is that all forms of Sinspar behave alike. They do not. Though all deaden Resonance, each species-born formation carries its own harmonic temperament, and to ignore that difference is to invite failure.  

## Legacy

Harmonic Sinspar endures because necessity is stronger than disgust. So long as [Resonance|url] remains a force capable of wonder and ruin in equal measure, there will always be need for a material that can swallow its excess. And so mankind returns, time and again, to the same shameful truth:   Some of the safest chambers ever built were carved from what the Myrkild themselves had no use for.
