> The world does not always break beneath the Wyrdweave. Sometimes it is whipped into remembering.
> 
> — Attributed to an early Pathfinder

  _Tideglass is not a gemstone. It is the fossilised aftermath of a Wyrdweave flare._   Tideglass is not mined as one would extract ore, nor cultivated as Harmonic Sinspar is grown, nor awakened and bound like [Galdyr / Galdrvyr](/w/viscerium/a/galdyr-2F-galdrvyr-material). It is a scar left upon [[Lore/Degel System/Errack]] by the violence of The Wyrdweave itself. When the Wyrdweave convulses in rare cosmic upheaval, its force lashes across the surface of the world and vitrifies whatever lies beneath that passing wound. Stone, ash, sand, and silicate-rich earth are seized, dragged, and frozen into black glass that retains the afterimage of that motion. The resulting structures erupt from the ground in jagged sprays, hooked fins, and frozen arcs, as though a violent splash of molten crystal had been halted mid-motion. Despite their beauty, Tideglass formations are widely regarded as dangerous relics of catastrophic cosmic events.  

## Formation

Tideglass is formed not by [Resonants](/w/viscerium/a/resonance-article), nor by battlefield excess, but by rare fluctuations in the Wyrdweave on a scale far beyond mortal will. When such disturbances strike Errack, the land is not neatly cratered or blasted into tidy rings. Instead it is struck in long directional lashes of force.  
  For this reason Tideglass rarely forms bowls, pits, or impact circles. It forms scars.  
A true Tideglass site appears as a drawn wound through the land: black glass rising in wave-like ridges, slivered combs, and sweeping arcs that all lean in the direction of the force that created them. The ground surrounding these formations often appears peeled or dragged aside, as though the surface of the world briefly softened beneath the blow before being forced to preserve the shape of its pain.  
  Within the troughs and depressions of these scar-fields, fracture ripples often run in parallel or hooked lines, preserving the aftershock of the event like a shudder trapped in stone.  
Many formations resemble:

- frozen splashes of liquid glass
- stretched crystalline ribbons
- curling wave crests
- elongated arcs drawn through the earth

  Some appear as though a whip of molten glass struck the land and solidified in the instant of impact.   Traversing such fields is perilous. Though Tideglass possesses great strength, it is also extremely brittle. Thin sheets between larger shards may fracture without warning, sending storms of razor splinters through the air. In some formations translucent membranes of glass stretch between crystal spines like shattered curtains or frozen waves. These surfaces shimmer faintly and often shatter under the slightest strain.    

## Appearance

Tideglass fractures conchoidally, splitting in smooth shell-like curves with edges keen enough to open flesh by accident.  
In hand, unworked pieces are black or near-black, though the darkness is seldom dead. At the right angle, the astral fire within turns slowly beneath the surface like colour seen through smoked ice.  
Its internal structure is chaotic and imperfect, formed from violently fused mineral material rather than slow crystallisation.  
  Typical features include;

- smoky ultramarine glass,
- black-blue obsidian inclusions,
- trapped mineral streaks,
- cloudy internal zones,
- fracture lines,
- gas bubbles

The material often resembles a hybrid of impact glass, obsidian, and tourmaline, though far more fractured and irregular. At the base of most formations the material becomes extremely dark and opaque, almost indistinguishable from vitrified slag.  

### Dichroic Colouration

The strange colours sometimes seen within Tideglass are not pigments.  
Instead they arise from microscopic layered structures within the glass that bend and divide incoming light. These thin films create interference patterns that only appear when viewed from particular angles.  
Because of this, Tideglass often appears dull or nearly colourless until light strikes it correctly.  
When it does, colour manifests in three ways:

- Thin edges flash briefly with crimson, magenta, or violet.
- Angled crystal faces display oil-slick interference sheens of cyan, gold, and teal.
- Deep within certain formations, faint drifting lights may be seen moving slowly through the cloudy glass.

  These inner motes are widely believed to be residual resonance energy trapped during the formation of the material.

   

## Harmonic Character

Tideglass does not behave as [Galdyr / Galdrvyr](/w/viscerium/a/galdyr-2F-galdrvyr-material) does, nor as Harmonic Sinspar does. Galdyr is primordial matter capable of true Attunement, forming symbiotic bonds with a Resonant wielder.  
Harmonic Sinspar is a suppressive crystal born of Myrkild refuse and drowned harmonics, created to smother coherent song.  
Tideglass is neither conductor nor suppressor, the material remembers the passage of a Wyrdweave event upon the world. When exposed to nearby Resonance, the colours within the glass may brighten, recoil, align, or drift in subtle response.  
Not because Resonants created it, but because their art is itself a lesser and more wasteful interaction with the same underlying fabric.    

## Awakening

Of all Tideglass’ properties, none is more feared or more coveted than its role in the emergence of certain Resonants.  
If successfully ingested under the right and most perilous conditions, Tideglass may impress its wave-like geometry upon the body of one already standing upon the threshold of awakening. In such rare survivors, the material’s pattern is believed to seed the modified dermal papillae layer for which awakened Resonants are known, mapping the flesh into a form more receptive to harmonic expression.   This is no gentle rite. Most who attempt it gain nothing but torn throat, ruptured gut, fever, blood loss or death. Many charlatans have sold common black glass to the desperate. Others have killed fools with the genuine article at the wrong hour. Tideglass does not grant Resonance from nothing. At best, it marks the ready. At worst, it leaves the unworthy dying beautifully.    

## Harvesting and Use

Harvesting Tideglass is dangerous precisely because its formations seldom sit in neat, singular deposits. A proper scar-field may run for many meters, surfacing and diving through the terrain in hooked lines and black crests. Strike the wrong point too hard and the fracture may carry down the whole lash, breaking it into storms of razor splinters.   For that reason, experienced cutters do not quarry Tideglass as they would common crystal. They read the scar. They follow its sweep, brace its ridges, and cut with the wound’s own logic rather than against it.   Beyond awakening rites, Tideglass is used in augury plates, ritual lenses, diagnostic slabs and certain observatory devices designed to study subtle changes in nearby Resonance. Thin polished sections are especially prized, for they show the slow astral whorls within with unsettling clarity.
