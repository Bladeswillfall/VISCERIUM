Strain pressure: Gluttony Silhouette: Towering stacks of fleshy trumpets and chimney-like tubes, some as tall as keeps, with wet concave mouths that glisten at sunrise. What they really are: Digestive air-organs anchored to a huge buried nutrient bladder. The visible towers are only feeding mouths and vent shafts. How they compete: They harvest everything—fog moisture, insects, carrion stink, windblown organic dust, nesting birds, even runoff from battlefields. Gluttony already carries the language of pits, parasites, hunger, and devouring, so a megaflora that feeds through open throats fits cleanly. Behaviour: At dawn the mouths flex open and release warm vapour; at dusk they draw inward and become almost still. Use: The inner condensate is harvested for corrosive alchemy, tanning, and corpse reduction. Fear: A valley full of them sounds like breathing.
