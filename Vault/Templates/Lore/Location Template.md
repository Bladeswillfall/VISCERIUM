> [!authoring]- Need a hand?
>
> **Developing this location**
> Use only the questions that reveal something useful. These are development prompts, not required article headings.
>
> **Place and function**
> - Why does this place exist here rather than somewhere else?
> - What terrain, water, climate, route, resource, hazard, border, or historical decision most strongly controls how it functions?
> - How do people, goods, information, armies, animals, waste, water, or energy move through it?
> - What important dependency would fail first if the place were isolated?
>
> **People and ordinary life**
> - What do residents consider completely normal here that an outsider would notice?
> - Where are work, wealth, poverty, authority, worship, danger, leisure, and status visible in the physical environment?
> - What do ordinary people complain about, depend on, fear, celebrate, or take pride in?
>
> **History in the fabric**
> - What existed here before the current inhabitants or use?
> - What survives visibly, even when its original purpose or builders are forgotten?
> - What has been buried, reused, fortified, extended, repaired, cannibalised, sealed, or built over?
> - Which road, wall, foundation, ruin, district boundary, place-name, shrine, scar, or service still forces the present to accommodate the past?
>
> **VISCERIUM spatial lenses**
> - Layer the world: later eras should inherit earlier structures instead of replacing them cleanly.
> - Use verticality when walls, terrain, safety, density, industry, ruins, or infrastructure constrain outward growth.
> - Let threat shape form: warfare, Myrkild activity, environmental danger, policing, or scarcity should alter movement and construction where relevant.
> - Make Resonance material: what was built, strengthened, distorted, regulated, feared, ritualised, exploited, or abandoned because Resonance existed?
> - Look for obsolete infrastructure given new purposes rather than removed for convenience.
>
> **Environment and ecology**
> - Which seasonal change, fauna, flora, fungi, pollution, extraction, or ecological dependency actually affects travel, settlement, work, food, health, or culture?
> - What draws people here despite the hazards?
>
> **Article shape**
> - Use landmarks and districts only when they help the reader navigate, understand identity, or see a pressure made physical.
> - Do not quarantine all history under `History`; explain an old wall while discussing the modern district if the wall still shapes it.
> - A location article may ultimately be organised around its distinctive places or pressures rather than `Geography / Culture / History`.
>
> **Compose the article**
> - Do not answer these prompts one-by-one.
> - Choose the few ideas that actually distinguish this subject.
> - Group facts by cause, consequence, experience, or tension rather than by questionnaire category.
> - Rename, merge, reorder, or remove the fallback headings when a stronger reader-facing structure emerges.
> - Put history where its consequences are felt; use a dedicated history section only for chronology that still needs one.
> - Use wikilinks as depth valves: link to connected systems, people, places, practices, and events instead of re-explaining them here.
> - Let local voices, notices, prices, jokes, graffiti, complaints, crude detail, and contradictory testimony interrupt polished exposition when they reveal people or institutions; do not polish away the people.
>

> **Before publishing**
> - Keep map metadata only when this place belongs on a configured map.
> - Use `minZoom` / `maxZoom` only when marker visibility should change with map scale.
> - Write a reader-safe `description`.
> - Establish the relevant era or context.
> - Set `published` only when the article first becomes public.
> - Leave optional metadata absent until it improves navigation, presentation, or continuity.
>
> **Boundary**
> This helper is creator guidance, not canon. Do not use it as a secrecy boundary; unrevealed material still belongs in `Vault/Private`.
