> [!authoring]- Need a hand?
>
> **Developing this item**
> Use only the questions that reveal something useful. These are development prompts, not required article headings.
>
> **Purpose and use**
> - What problem does this object solve, and for whom?
> - What does it enable that would otherwise be difficult, dangerous, expensive, illegal, sacred, prestigious, or impossible?
> - Which limitation, maintenance need, failure mode, training requirement, or trade-off matters in actual use?
>
> **Construction and support**
> - Which materials or components meaningfully define the object?
> - Who can manufacture, maintain, repair, modify, fuel, recharge, feed, calibrate, or safely operate it?
> - Which supply chain, workshop, guild, institution, industrial base, Galdyr craft, or specialist knowledge makes it possible?
>
> **Availability and meaning**
> - Who can legally or practically obtain it, and who cannot?
> - What does ownership communicate about profession, wealth, faction, class, faith, office, criminality, or status?
> - Is scarcity created by materials, skill, law, logistics, secrecy, ritual, monopoly, or danger?
>
> **History and inheritance**
> - What earlier design, technology, craft tradition, weapon, tool, or ritual object led to this one?
> - What has been repaired, standardised, improvised, miniaturised, industrialised, ritualised, repurposed, or misunderstood over time?
> - Does a later era still use an older object because infrastructure, doctrine, symbolism, scarcity, or simple durability keeps it relevant?
>
> **VISCERIUM lenses**
> - Treat technology as material culture: objects should imply labour, institutions, supply, maintenance, status, doctrine, and everyday habits.
> - If Resonance or Galdyr matters, ask what the object requires from a Resonant, what it changes around itself, who controls access, and what failure costs.
> - Avoid exact performance statistics unless a current creative decision genuinely depends on them.
>
> **Compose the article**
> - Do not answer these prompts one-by-one.
> - Choose the few ideas that actually distinguish this subject.
> - Group facts by cause, consequence, experience, or tension rather than by questionnaire category.
> - Rename, merge, reorder, or remove the fallback headings when a stronger reader-facing structure emerges.
> - Put history where its consequences are felt; use a dedicated history section only for chronology that still needs one.
> - Use wikilinks as depth valves: link to connected systems, people, places, practices, and events instead of re-explaining them here.
> - Let local voices, notices, prices, jokes, graffiti, complaints, crude detail, and contradictory testimony interrupt polished exposition when they reveal people or institutions; do not polish away the people.
>
> **Go deeper when needed**

>
> **Before publishing**
> - Use `item_type` for a broad practical class such as weapon, armour, tool, vehicle, technology, or artefact.
> - Write a reader-safe `description`.
> - Establish the relevant era or context.
> - Set `published` only when the article first becomes public.
> - Leave optional metadata absent until it improves navigation, presentation, or continuity.
>
> **Boundary**
> This helper is creator guidance, not canon. Do not use it as a secrecy boundary; unrevealed material still belongs in `Vault/Private`.
