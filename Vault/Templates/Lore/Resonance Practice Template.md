> **Developing this Resonance practice or tradition**
> Use only the questions that reveal something useful. These are development prompts, not required article headings.
>
> **Principle and method**
> - What aspect of Resonance does this practice exploit or interpret?
> - What must a Resonant actually do, perceive, endure, prepare, or understand?
> - What conditions, materials, Galdyr, environments, emotional states, training, or collaborators does it require?
>
> **Capabilities and limits**
> - What can it do reliably?
> - What can it appear to do but not actually guarantee?
> - Where does it fail, and what does failure cost the Resonant, subject, environment, infrastructure, or bystanders?
> - Which countermeasures, conditions, exhaustion, uncertainty, range, attention, social control, or material limits keep it from solving every problem?
>
> **Training and knowledge**
> - How is it learned, demonstrated, tested, transmitted, licensed, inherited, hidden, or rediscovered?
> - Who is allowed to teach or practise it?
> - What knowledge is missing, misunderstood, dangerous, ritualised, or deliberately suppressed?
>
> **Institution and society**
> - Who employs, controls, regulates, worships, fears, criminalises, medicalises, commercialises, weaponises, or monopolises the practice?
> - How does it alter ordinary work, architecture, warfare, medicine, transport, law, religion, class, or family life where relevant?
> - What does the public think practitioners can do that they actually cannot?
>
> **Material culture**
> - What buildings, tools, Galdyr, protective measures, marks, uniforms, workshops, schools, prisons, shrines, infrastructure, or ruins exist because of this practice?
> - What remains physically visible after the practitioners are gone?
>
> **History and inheritance**
> - Who first formalised, discovered, mythologised, industrialised, militarised, or prohibited the practice?
> - How did its meaning or use change between eras?
> - Which later technology or institution is built on a Resonant practice whose origin has been forgotten or politically rewritten?
>
> **VISCERIUM lenses**
> - Treat Resonance as a force that changes civilisation, not merely a character ability.
> - Prefer consequences, institutions, material traces, limits, and cultural responses over pseudo-scientific explanation.
> - Different cultures can interpret the same underlying phenomenon in incompatible ways without requiring multiple unrelated magic systems.
>
> **Compose the article**
> - Do not answer these prompts one-by-one.
> - Choose the few ideas that actually distinguish this subject.
> - Group facts by cause, consequence, experience, or tension rather than by questionnaire category.
> - Rename, merge, reorder, or remove the fallback headings when a stronger reader-facing structure emerges.
> - Put history where its consequences are felt; use a dedicated history section only for chronology that still needs one.
> - Use wikilinks as depth valves: link to connected systems, people, places, practices, and events instead of re-explaining them here.
> - Let local voices, notices, prices, jokes, graffiti, complaints, crude detail, and contradictory testimony interrupt polished exposition when they reveal people or institutions; do not polish away the people.
