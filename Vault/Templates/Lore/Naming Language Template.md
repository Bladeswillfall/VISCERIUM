> **Developing this naming language**
> Use only the questions that reveal something useful. These are development prompts, not required article headings.
>
> **Purpose**
> - What cultures, regions, factions, eras, or communities use this naming system?
> - Do you need a full language, or only enough structure to make names feel related and repeatable?
> - Which existing names already define the sound and should be treated as anchors?
>
> **Sound**
> - Which consonants and vowels recur?
> - Which sounds are rare or absent?
> - What syllable shapes, consonant clusters, stress patterns, endings, or rhythms make a name recognisable?
>
> **Construction**
> - Which roots, prefixes, suffixes, compounds, particles, honorifics, patronymics, matronymics, clan markers, occupational terms, or religious elements recur?
> - How do personal names differ from place names, titles, objects, events, ships, military units, or institutions?
> - What grammatical complexity can be omitted because it does not help naming?
>
> **Social variation**
> - How do class, gender, region, profession, lineage, religion, ethnicity, conquest, migration, or assimilation change naming practice?
> - Which names sound archaic, foreign, prestigious, rustic, criminal, religious, aristocratic, military, or fashionable to speakers?
>
> **Historical change**
> - How do names shift between CITADEL, SMOG, NEARSIGHT, and ENTROPY?
> - Which older roots survive in place names after ordinary speech changes?
> - What gets shortened, respelled, translated, misheard, bureaucratised, or revived?
>
> **Build an approved bank**
> - Keep a small set of roots, affixes, example names, place-name elements, titles, and common phrases that can generate future names consistently.
> - Record why examples fit the pattern instead of only accumulating a list.
>
> **Compose the article**
> - Do not answer these prompts one-by-one.
> - Choose the few ideas that actually distinguish this subject.
> - Group facts by cause, consequence, experience, or tension rather than by questionnaire category.
> - Rename, merge, reorder, or remove the fallback headings when a stronger reader-facing structure emerges.
> - Put history where its consequences are felt; use a dedicated history section only for chronology that still needs one.
> - Use wikilinks as depth valves: link to connected systems, people, places, practices, and events instead of re-explaining them here.
> - Let local voices, notices, prices, jokes, graffiti, complaints, crude detail, and contradictory testimony interrupt polished exposition when they reveal people or institutions; do not polish away the people.
>
