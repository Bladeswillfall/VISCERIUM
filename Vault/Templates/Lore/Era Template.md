>
> **Developing this era**
> Use only the questions that reveal something useful. These are development prompts, not required article headings.
>
> **Inherited world**
> - What physical, political, religious, cultural, ecological, and technological inheritance does this era receive from the one before it?
> - Which old systems still work, which survive only as habits or ruins, and which have been given new purposes?
> - What do people in this era take for granted that earlier people would find remarkable?
>
> **Defining conditions**
> - Which technologies, social assumptions, institutions, conflicts, environments, threats, and everyday realities define the baseline of life?
> - What does an ordinary day feel like for people far from the era's famous events?
> - Which opportunities and fears are newly normal?
>
> **Change and pressure**
> - What is becoming harder to sustain?
> - Which institutions, technologies, beliefs, resources, wars, migrations, Resonant practices, or Myrkild pressures are pushing the world toward something new?
> - Which people benefit from the era's dominant systems, and who is trapped beneath them?
>
> **Memory and perspective**
> - How do later eras romanticise, simplify, condemn, exploit, or misunderstand this period?
> - Which achievements survive more visibly than their human cost?
> - Which ordinary practices disappear so completely that later people struggle to interpret their remains?
>
> **Legacy**
> - What infrastructure, borders, ruins, languages, institutions, doctrines, customs, traumas, myths, technologies, and ecological changes does this era leave behind?
> - What problem does the next era inherit without understanding its original cause?
>
> **VISCERIUM lenses**
> - Treat CITADEL → SMOG → NEARSIGHT → ENTROPY as a continuous material world, not four aesthetic resets.
> - Show old buildings inside new cities, old roads under new transport networks, old beliefs inside modern institutions, and obsolete tools reused under new pressures.
> - The era page establishes a baseline; individual places and factions should still vary.
>
> **Compose the article**
> - Do not answer these prompts one-by-one.
> - Choose the few ideas that actually distinguish this subject.
> - Group facts by cause, consequence, experience, or tension rather than by questionnaire category.
> - Rename, merge, reorder, or remove the fallback headings when a stronger reader-facing structure emerges.
> - Put history where its consequences are felt; use a dedicated history section only for chronology that still needs one.
> - Use wikilinks as depth valves: link to connected systems, people, places, practices, and events instead of re-explaining them here.
> - Let local voices, notices, prices, jokes, graffiti, complaints, crude detail, and contradictory testimony interrupt polished exposition when they reveal people or institutions; do not polish away the people.
>
> **Go deeper when needed**
> - [[Support/VISCERIUM Worldbuilding Heuristics|VISCERIUM Worldbuilding Heuristics]]
> - [[Support/Lore Composition SOP|Lore Composition SOP]]
>
> **Before publishing**
> - Era boundaries use the canonical calendar engine.
> - Set `allowGapAfter: true` only when the gap before the next era is deliberate.
> - Assign `eraId` deliberately before adding the generated timeline block.
> - Write a reader-safe `description`.
> - Establish the relevant era or context.
> - Set `published` only when the article first becomes public.
> - Leave optional metadata absent until it improves navigation, presentation, or continuity.
>
> **Boundary**
> This helper is creator guidance, not canon. Do not use it as a secrecy boundary; unrevealed material still belongs in `Vault/Private`.
