> [!authoring]- Need a hand?
>
> **Developing this faction**
> Use only the questions that reveal something useful. These are development prompts, not required article headings.
>
> **Identity and membership**
> - What story does this faction tell about itself, and what would an outsider notice that complicates that story?
> - Who counts as a member, citizen, subject, adherent, employee, client, or dependent?
> - How does somebody join, belong, advance, leave, defect, or get excluded when those distinctions matter?
>
> **Power**
> - Who officially holds authority, and who can actually make or obstruct decisions?
> - What gives that authority legitimacy: law, tradition, faith, wealth, force, knowledge, patronage, Resonance, dependency, or fear?
> - Who can challenge a decision, and what does that challenge cost?
> - Where do internal factions, classes, regions, generations, professions, or institutions disagree?
>
> **Culture and ordinary life**
> - What behaviour earns respect, shame, suspicion, protection, or punishment?
> - What does the faction claim to value, and what does ordinary behaviour reveal it actually rewards?
> - What does family, work, education, worship, recreation, status, and service look like for ordinary people where relevant?
> - How are outsiders, dissidents, rival cultures, atypical people, and Resonants treated?
>
> **Material base**
> - What keeps this faction materially alive?
> - What can it produce or control unusually well, what must it import, and who performs the labour?
> - Which route, resource, institution, technology, specialist knowledge, or infrastructure would hurt most if lost?
> - Who benefits from the current system, and who bears its hidden costs?
>
> **Force and security**
> - What does this faction believe wins wars or preserves order?
> - Who is expected to fight, police, supply, build, heal, or endure?
> - Which terrain, logistics, political obligations, industry, or social limits stop it from using force however it wants?
>
> **History, memory, and inheritance**
> - Which events still explain present behaviour, territory, institutions, prejudice, alliances, rituals, or fears?
> - What has this faction inherited from predecessor cultures or states even when it no longer admits the origin?
> - Where do official history, public memory, and established canon differ?
>
> **VISCERIUM lenses**
> - Let war, Myrkild pressure, Resonance, scarcity, and geography reshape institutions rather than exist as separate lore categories.
> - Show continuity between eras through inherited law, infrastructure, ritual, language, military doctrine, ruins, and obsolete systems given new purposes.
> - Preserve contradiction: a coherent society can contain hypocrisies, competing values, regional differences, and people who do not fit its ideal.
>
> **Compose the article**
> - Do not answer these prompts one-by-one.
> - Choose the few ideas that actually distinguish this subject.
> - Group facts by cause, consequence, experience, or tension rather than by questionnaire category.
> - Rename, merge, reorder, or remove the fallback headings when a stronger reader-facing structure emerges.
> - Put history where its consequences are felt; use a dedicated history section only for chronology that still needs one.
> - Use wikilinks as depth valves: link to connected systems, people, places, practices, and events instead of re-explaining them here.
> - Let local voices, notices, prices, jokes, graffiti, complaints, crude detail, and contradictory testimony interrupt polished exposition when they reveal people or institutions; do not polish away the people.
>

> - Write a reader-safe `description`.
> - Establish the relevant era or context.
> - Set `published` only when the article first becomes public.
> - Leave optional metadata absent until it improves navigation, presentation, or continuity.
>
