> [!authoring]- Need a hand?
>
> **Developing this event**
> Use only the questions that reveal something useful. These are development prompts, not required article headings.
>
> **Start with change**
> - What changed because this happened?
> - Who wanted what before the event, and what did they actually get?
> - Why did this happen here and now rather than earlier, later, or elsewhere?
>
> **Causes and course**
> - Which pressures, decisions, accidents, shortages, beliefs, rivalries, technologies, terrain, or prior events made it possible?
> - Which moments changed the direction or meaning of the event?
> - What did participants misunderstand while it was happening?
>
> **Consequences**
> - What changed immediately for people, territory, institutions, infrastructure, culture, economy, environment, or military balance where relevant?
> - Which consequences outlived the participants?
> - What result differed from the stated intention?
>
> **Record and memory**
> - What physical, documentary, oral, Resonant, ecological, or archaeological evidence survives?
> - What did witnesses or contemporaries believe happened?
> - What do later scholars, institutions, propagandists, faiths, or communities claim happened?
> - What has been lost, falsified, mythologised, censored, misdated, or rendered ambiguous by time?
> - What remains genuinely unknown?
>
> **VISCERIUM lenses**
> - Let events leave material scars: ruined routes, reused forts, altered borders, memorials, contaminated land, new rituals, changed names, laws, grudges, technologies, or demographics.
> - Track inheritance across eras: a CITADEL event can survive into SMOG or NEARSIGHT as infrastructure, doctrine, myth, prejudice, disputed evidence, or a problem nobody remembers creating.
> - History should not become omniscient merely because the Codex records it; distinguish established canon from what people inside Errack know.
>
> **Compose the article**
> - Do not answer these prompts one-by-one.
> - Choose the few ideas that actually distinguish this subject.
> - Group facts by cause, consequence, experience, or tension rather than by questionnaire category.
> - Rename, merge, reorder, or remove the fallback headings when a stronger reader-facing structure emerges.
> - Put history where its consequences are felt; use a dedicated history section only for chronology that still needs one.
> - Use wikilinks as depth valves: link to connected systems, people, places, practices, and events instead of re-explaining them here.
> - Let local voices, notices, prices, jokes, graffiti, complaints, crude detail, and contradictory testimony interrupt polished exposition when they reveal people or institutions; do not polish away the people.
>
> **Go deeper when needed**
> - [[System/SOPs/Sourcebook Readiness SOP|Sourcebook Readiness SOP]] when consequences need continuity testing
> - [[Support/VISCERIUM Worldbuilding Heuristics|VISCERIUM Worldbuilding Heuristics]]
> - [[Support/Lore Composition SOP|Lore Composition SOP]]
>
> **Before publishing**
> - `calendarDate` is the single chronology source. Do not add legacy `timeline.year`, `timeline.date`, or `timeline.id` fields.
> - Add `calendarEndDate` only for a genuine ranged event or period.
> - Write a reader-safe `description`.
> - Establish the relevant era or context.
> - Set `published` only when the article first becomes public.
> - Leave optional metadata absent until it improves navigation, presentation, or continuity.
>
> **Boundary**
> This helper is creator guidance, not canon. Do not use it as a secrecy boundary; unrevealed material still belongs in `Vault/Private`.
