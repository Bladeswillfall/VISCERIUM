>
> **Developing this culture**
> Use only the questions that reveal something useful. These are development prompts, not required article headings.
>
> **Worldview**
> - What does this culture consider a good life?
> - What behaviour earns respect, shame, suspicion, protection, or ridicule?
> - What does the culture say about morality, community, work, property, conflict, nature, family, love, duty, and identity where those beliefs actually affect behaviour?
>
> **Social order**
> - What does a household or family actually look like?
> - How do age, class, gender, profession, wealth, lineage, region, citizenship, faith, or Resonance status change expectations where relevant?
> - How easy is it to move between social positions, and what makes that movement legitimate or threatening?
> - How are outsiders, foreigners, dissidents, atypical people, and people who reject expected roles treated?
>
> **Everyday life**
> - What do people eat, wear, build, repair, celebrate, collect, play, watch, sing, mourn, wash, teach, and complain about?
> - What work sustains ordinary households?
> - What changes between elites, ordinary people, frontier communities, cities, villages, generations, or regions?
>
> **Ritual and custom**
> - What happens around birth, naming, adulthood, courtship, marriage, hospitality, oath-taking, illness, death, grief, victory, failure, and seasonal change?
> - Which customs are formal ideals and which are what people actually do?
> - What taboo is socially powerful even when no written law enforces it?
>
> **Knowledge, belief, and Resonance**
> - What knowledge is common, prestigious, secret, restricted, gendered, professional, sacred, or distrusted?
> - How does this culture explain Resonance, and what uses are admired, tolerated, licensed, feared, hidden, ritualised, or forbidden?
> - Which institutions gain power from controlling knowledge, education, ritual, medicine, media, archives, or Resonants?
>
> **Contradiction and inheritance**
> - What does this culture misunderstand about itself?
> - Which values conflict with one another in ordinary life?
> - What survives from older cultures, conquered peoples, migrations, religions, technologies, or previous eras?
> - Which old custom still exists after its original reason has been forgotten or replaced?
>
> **Material expression**
> - How do architecture, clothing, food, public space, tools, decoration, burial, music, naming, military dress, transport, and household objects make the culture visible without exposition?
> - Where do scarcity, climate, threat, labour, religion, Resonance, and historical layering explain an aesthetic choice?
>
> **Compose the article**
> - Do not answer these prompts one-by-one.
> - Choose the few ideas that actually distinguish this subject.
> - Group facts by cause, consequence, experience, or tension rather than by questionnaire category.
> - Rename, merge, reorder, or remove the fallback headings when a stronger reader-facing structure emerges.
> - Put history where its consequences are felt; use a dedicated history section only for chronology that still needs one.
> - Use wikilinks as depth valves: link to connected systems, people, places, practices, and events instead of re-explaining them here.
> - Let local voices, notices, prices, jokes, graffiti, complaints, crude detail, and contradictory testimony interrupt polished exposition when they reveal people or institutions; do not polish away the people.
>
