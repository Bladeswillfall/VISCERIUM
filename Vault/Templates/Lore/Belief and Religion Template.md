>
> **Developing this belief or religion**
> Use only the questions that reveal something useful. These are development prompts, not required article headings.
>
> **Core belief**
> - What does this tradition claim is fundamentally true about reality, life, death, fate, divinity, spirits, ancestors, Resonance, or the human place in the world?
> - What does it say a good person should become or do?
> - Which questions does it leave deliberately unanswered?
>
> **Myth and sacred focus**
> - Which creation stories, heroes, catastrophes, revelations, ancestors, gods, spirits, places, objects, texts, relics, or natural phenomena carry authority?
> - Which stories are literal doctrine, symbolic teaching, local folklore, or disputed interpretation?
>
> **Practice**
> - What does an ordinary believer actually do daily, seasonally, at home, in public, and during crisis?
> - What marks birth, naming, adulthood, marriage, oath, illness, death, mourning, pilgrimage, victory, guilt, or purification where relevant?
> - Which foods, clothes, gestures, places, times, objects, substances, or behaviours become sacred or taboo?
>
> **Institution and authority**
> - Who interprets doctrine, controls sacred property, teaches, judges, heals, initiates, records, or excommunicates?
> - How does somebody join, leave, convert, inherit membership, or become a specialist?
> - What gives religious authority legitimacy, and what happens when local practice disagrees with official teaching?
>
> **Resonance**
> - Is Resonance evidence of divinity, a natural force, contamination, temptation, gift, profession, sacrament, or something else?
> - Which Resonant practices are sacred, tolerated, regulated, monopolised, feared, or forbidden?
> - What material institutions or rituals exist because Resonance is real?
>
> **Difference and conflict**
> - How are non-believers, heretics, rival sects, converts, syncretic communities, and doubters treated?
> - Where do class, region, gender, profession, generation, politics, or historical trauma produce different versions of the same belief?
> - Which doctrine is most often compromised in ordinary life?
>
> **History and inheritance**
> - What older belief did this tradition absorb, erase, rename, reinterpret, or condemn?
> - Which schism, persecution, reform, miracle claim, war, migration, discovery, or political bargain still shapes practice?
> - What later generations believe about the tradition may differ from what its founders intended.
>
> **Compose the article**
> - Do not answer these prompts one-by-one.
> - Choose the few ideas that actually distinguish this subject.
> - Group facts by cause, consequence, experience, or tension rather than by questionnaire category.
> - Rename, merge, reorder, or remove the fallback headings when a stronger reader-facing structure emerges.
> - Put history where its consequences are felt; use a dedicated history section only for chronology that still needs one.
> - Use wikilinks as depth valves: link to connected systems, people, places, practices, and events instead of re-explaining them here.
> - Let local voices, notices, prices, jokes, graffiti, complaints, crude detail, and contradictory testimony interrupt polished exposition when they reveal people or institutions; do not polish away the people.
