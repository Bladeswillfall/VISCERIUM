> [!authoring]- Need a hand?
>
> **Developing this species**
> Use only the questions that reveal something useful. These are development prompts, not required article headings.
>
> **Identity**
> - What makes this species recognisably Errackian rather than an Earth organism with a new name?
> - What is the quickest reliable way an inhabitant could identify it?
> - Which trait actually matters to ecology, survival, culture, trade, medicine, warfare, or story?
>
> **Ecology and behaviour**
> - What does it eat, compete with, depend on, shelter, spread, hunt, or get hunted by?
> - What conditions limit where it can live?
> - What seasonal, social, reproductive, migratory, defensive, or predatory behaviour changes how people encounter it?
>
> **Relationship with people**
> - Why would an ordinary person hunt, farm, gather, cultivate, protect, avoid, destroy, study, fear, or revere it?
> - What products, hazards, labour, medicines, foods, materials, beliefs, or livelihoods depend on it?
> - How has domestication, exploitation, pollution, war, Resonance, or Myrkild activity changed its behaviour or distribution?
>
> **Culture and interpretation**
> - Which culture gives it a symbolic, ritual, taboo, medicinal, military, or religious meaning, and why?
> - Do different regions or classes understand the same species differently?
> - Which local names or folk beliefs are useful because they reveal a relationship rather than merely add vocabulary?
>
> **VISCERIUM lenses**
> - Treat ecology as a network of consequences rather than an isolated bestiary entry.
> - If the species is Myrkildic, show consequences beyond combat: travel, agriculture, settlement, burial, architecture, labour, religion, policing, or childhood behaviour.
> - Prefer one useful constraint or ecological dependency over exhaustive anatomy.
>
> **Compose the article**
> - Do not answer these prompts one-by-one.
> - Choose the few ideas that actually distinguish this subject.
> - Group facts by cause, consequence, experience, or tension rather than by questionnaire category.
> - Rename, merge, reorder, or remove the fallback headings when a stronger reader-facing structure emerges.
> - Put history where its consequences are felt; use a dedicated history section only for chronology that still needs one.
> - Use wikilinks as depth valves: link to connected systems, people, places, practices, and events instead of re-explaining them here.
> - Let local voices, notices, prices, jokes, graffiti, complaints, crude detail, and contradictory testimony interrupt polished exposition when they reveal people or institutions; do not polish away the people.
>

> **Before publishing**
> - Use `species_kind` only for a useful broad category.
> - Write a reader-safe `description`.
> - Establish the relevant era or context.
> - Set `published` only when the article first becomes public.
> - Leave optional metadata absent until it improves navigation, presentation, or continuity.
>
> **Boundary**
> This helper is creator guidance, not canon. Do not use it as a secrecy boundary; unrevealed material still belongs in `Vault/Private`.
